01 — Diagnosis

A product that talks about machines to people who run a business

The navigable objects today are controls, serials, faults and configuration fields. The dealer's actual business is organised around households — who owns what, how old it is, what's still covered, and who is due for a conversation. The product has no representation of that at all.

  • 01Opportunities and alerts don't speak. 104 opportunities sit in a tab with no visible connection to the 96 alerts that would justify any of them.
  • 02There is no book. Customers are reachable only by name search or by drilling from a control. No portfolio view, no sort by value, no sense of a whole.
  • 03Wear data exists but is never commercial. 16,504 blower hours on one system is a real signal of age. It's shown as a counter.
  • 04The predictive engine finds billable work — slow leaks, degrading units — and presents it as an engineering result.
  • 05Nothing shows warranty, install date, agreement status, or equipment age. The four things a commercial conversation depends on.
02 — The bet
Dealers engage with money, not machines. The unit of value is the customer relationship, not the control.

Serial numbers become an attribute of a household. Faults and predictions become inputs to a commercial statement: this leak is a billable visit; this fifteen-year-old unit throwing repeat compressor faults is a replacement conversation.

The tradeoff, stated plainly

This is the highest-investment option and the only one that can fail on data rather than on design. It depends on install dates, warranty terms, pricing and agreement status — much of it in FSM systems of varying quality, or a filing cabinet, or nowhere. Wrong dollar figures destroy credibility faster than no dollar figures. The money rule below makes that survivable; it does not make it disappear.

03 — The money rule

Every figure carries its provenance and degrades rather than guesses

This is the load-bearing wall of Option B. Design should treat it as a component system, not a set of caveats bolted on late.

Hard rule 01

No point estimates from estimated data

$6,800–$9,200 is honest. $8,043 is a lie with decimal places.

Hard rule 02

Never total across mixed tiers silently

A portfolio figure summing measured and estimated must expose the split, not blend them into one confident number.

Hard rule 03

Zero is not unknown

A household with no pricing data isn't worth nothing; it's unmeasured. Conflating them is the fastest way to make a dealer distrust the screen.

Hard rule 04

Provenance is one tap away

Any figure can be interrogated: what went into this, when was it updated, what would improve it.

Hard rule 05

Absent data is an invitation

Empty states are the growth mechanism. Every "add your labour rate to see this" is an enrichment prompt.

And no black boxes

Health is composed, never a score

An opaque number will be distrusted by exactly the people whose trust this needs. Five legible factors instead — age, fault burden, performance trend, coverage, relationship — each verifiable against what a dealer already knows.

    05 — Defensibility

    The homeowner is in the room

    Dealers will turn the screen around. In a kitchen at 6pm, "let me show you what your system's been doing" is the most natural move available — so this becomes a customer-facing artifact whether it is designed as one or not.

    Design constraint

    Every figure, phrase and recommendation must be defensible to the homeowner if they read it over the dealer's shoulder. This is not a compliance appendix — it is the difference between a product Carrier can put its name on and one it cannot.

    Same moment, written two ways
    Rules out

    Target language

    No "ripe," no "warm lead," no urgency scoring presented as customer psychology. The customer is a customer, in every string, on every screen.

    Rules out

    Recommendations that hide the alternative

    A replacement recommendation must show repair beside it with real numbers — even when repair is the smaller sale.

    Requires

    Evidence before conclusion

    The fault history and performance trend that justify a recommendation are the most persuasive material available and the most defensible. Lead with them.

    Requires

    A narrower share artifact

    "Share with homeowner" outputs evidence and options only — no margin, no lifetime value, no pipeline framing. Demonstrated in the next section.

    06 — The moment

    Where the honesty is enforced

    A moment is a trigger, a recommended conversation, an honest set of options, supporting evidence, and a value with a confidence tier. Toggle the audience — internal regions disappear entirely from the homeowner artifact rather than being visually de-emphasised.

    07 — Concept model

    Four objects, one demotion

    Primary object

    Household

    A property, its occupants, its systems, its coverage, its history, its value. Replaces "control" as the thing the product is organised around.

    Attribute

    System

    The equipment set at a household. Age, model, refrigerant, runtime, connection. An attribute of a household, not a peer.

    Demoted

    Signal

    A fault, incident or prediction. An input, no longer an output. Signals never appear as the primary object anywhere in this product — they appear as evidence inside health and inside moments.

    New

    Moment

    A trigger, a recommended conversation, an honest set of options, supporting evidence, and a value with a confidence tier.

    Supersedes

    The existing Opportunities tab — 104 items, disconnected from everything — is replaced. Its contents migrate into moments or are retired. Running both is the fastest way to make neither trusted.

    08 — Screens

    Five surfaces, each in three data states

    8.1 — new home

    Book of Business

    Book header sentence, composition strip broken down by health factor (including an honest unmeasured segment), sortable household list, saved segments. The five counter tiles are removed — connected-control counts are platform metrics, not book metrics.

    8.2 — mobile matters

    Household record

    Identity, the five health factors, systems with age against service life, coverage in high contrast, open moments, one chronological history spine, and the config detail collapsed at the bottom. Will be opened in a truck and a hallway — design 390pt as a first-class case.

    8.3 — highest scrutiny

    Moment detail

    Trigger, evidence, options side by side, honest recommendation, value and confidence, actions. Charts are earned here and nowhere else: a capacity-decline curve is the most persuasive and most honest artifact available.

    8.4

    Pipeline

    Moments by lifecycle state, deferred items surfacing on their date, value totals with confidence splits intact. Deliberately not a CRM — see open question 06.

    8.5 — the growth engine

    Data health

    What's missing ranked by value unlocked, not a completeness percentage. Bulk enrichment, source and freshness per field, and in-place correction from anywhere. A dealer who can fix a wrong number stays; one who can only be annoyed by it leaves.

    Carried over

    The fault translation library

    Plain-language translation of every code remains a prerequisite, unchanged from the other options. Option B adds a second content layer on top: the commercial vocabulary.

    09 — Content

    Commercial vocabulary

    Voice rule

    Describe the situation, not the sale

    "This system is 15 years old and has had four faults this year" — not "high-value replacement opportunity."

    Voice rule

    Confidence in words, not only styling

    "Estimated from regional averages" appears as text, because styling doesn't survive a screenshot, a print, or a colourblind reader.

    Voice rule

    Recommendations name the tradeoff

    "Replacing now avoids a likely compressor failure and cuts running cost; repairing costs less today and buys perhaps three years."

    Voice rule

    Deferral is normal

    Copy should never imply a dealer is failing by deferring. Most moments are timing.

    11 — Readiness gates & sequencing

    This one can be built beautifully and still fail

    Unlike the other two options, Option B should not enter design production until the first two gates are answered. Both are knowable this week.

    Gate 1 — data

    How complete are install dates and warranty status?

    What percentage of connected households have a reliable install date and warranty status available to Carrier today? If it's low, Phase 1 is a data programme, not a design programme.

    Gate 2 — pricing

    Will dealers supply pricing and labour rates?

    Through what mechanism? Without this there is no measured tier and the entire product runs on ranges.

    Phase 1

    Make the book legible without money

    Household object, health factors, portfolio view, data health screen. Ships real value in the sparse state and drives the enrichment everything else depends on.

    Phase 2

    Coverage

    Warranty and agreement status, expiry moments, warranty capture. Highest-confidence data, lowest-risk figures, immediate dealer value.

    Phase 3

    Moments with money

    Estimated ranges first, measured values where pricing exists. The repair-vs-replace comparison and the share-with-homeowner artifact ship here, together — neither ships without the other.

    Phase 4

    Pipeline and attribution

    Lifecycle tracking, deferral, outcome capture, revenue attribution with confidence splits intact.

    The failure mode

    Shipping money before the data supports it. One dealer shown a confidently wrong replacement value in front of a customer will tell every dealer they know — and the platform does not get a second first impression.

    12 — Open questions

    Answer before design production

    1. What is the actual completeness of install date and warranty data across the connected base? Gate 1. Everything downstream is sized by this answer.
    2. Will dealers supply pricing and labour rates — and does Carrier want to hold them? Gate 2. Carries commercial sensitivity and possibly antitrust considerations worth a legal read.
    3. Does surfacing replacement opportunities create brand risk if a dealer uses it aggressively? Carrier's name is on the screen in the homeowner's kitchen. §05 is the design answer; a policy answer is also needed.
    4. Is the equipment-age-to-service-life model defensible per model family, or is it a generic curve? A generic curve applied to a specific serial is exactly the confident wrongness the money rule exists to prevent.
    5. Who is accountable if a figure shown to a homeowner is wrong? Needs a legal position before the share-with-homeowner artifact ships.
    6. Does this compete with the dealer's existing CRM, and does it need to? If larger dealers already run pipeline elsewhere, v1 may be better as a signal source that hands off than a pipeline that competes and loses.
