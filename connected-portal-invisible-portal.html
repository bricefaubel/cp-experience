01 — Diagnosis

A system of record that waits to be visited

Connected equipment produces exactly what a dealer's business runs on. The portal converts almost none of it into scheduled work. Low daily engagement is the symptom, not the problem.

  • 01Notifications default to None. All alert types, both email fields. The product cannot reach a dealer unless the dealer initiates contact.
  • 02Duplicates are not collapsed. Three identical zone communication faults inside one minute, each its own row in a 96-item list.
  • 03Faults ship as codes. 184-IDU DISSIP. BOARD COMM FAULT — no meaning, no cause, no action.
  • 04The predictive engine is manual. Malfunction Detection only runs when someone presses "Run a Report."
  • 05Diagnosis takes four screens, ending in ~50 raw config fields in flat alphabetical order.
02 — The bet
The right number of portal logins is zero. The signal should appear inside the software dealers already use all day.

Option A tries to earn the dealer's morning. This option refuses the premise: dealer attention is already spent inside an FSM, a dispatch board, a technician's phone. Put the intelligence where the attention already is.

The tradeoff, stated plainly

This trades relationship for reach. It delivers value to more dealers, faster, with less behaviour change than any alternative — and it moves Carrier one step further from the dealer, into the position of a data supplier inside someone else's software. Pro-tier merchandising, usage telemetry, and dealer influence all get harder. The countermeasures in §08 are real but partial.

Moves out of the portal

Triage and dispatch

Prioritisation and assignment happen in the dealer's FSM. This is the defining decision of Option C — and the one that is hardest to reverse.

Moves into Carrier's surface

The technician app

Diagnosis, check sequence, parts prediction, outcome capture. Under this strategy the app stops being a companion utility and becomes the product's centre of gravity.

Prerequisite — unavoidable

Grouping and plain language

Shared with Option A. Syndicating raw codes is worse than shipping nothing: it pollutes a partner's system and trains dispatchers to ignore Carrier records. Partner trust is spent once.

03 — Three tiers of control

Design does not own most of this experience

This strategy has a structural feature no normal PRD has: a meaningful share of the user-facing experience renders in software Carrier does not own and cannot design. Every surface decision must be explicit about which tier it sits in.

TierSurfacesDesign controlsMust survive
OwnedTechnician app, admin portal, fallback inbox, notification templatesEverything — layout, type, colour, motion, interactionNormal product constraints
InfluencedDeep-tier FSM rendering of a Carrier recordField structure, content, ordering, budgets, optional embedded viewHost typography, colour, layout, truncation
UncontrolledShallow FSM, email, printed tickets, SMS, whiteboardsWords onlyPlain text, no formatting whatsoever
The governing constraint

The diagnostic record must do its job when rendered as unformatted plain text in a 400-character field, and must do it better but not differently when rendered in a surface Carrier controls. Content hierarchy is carried by word order and sentence structure, not by type, colour, or layout. If the record only makes sense with a red badge next to it, it is designed wrong.

Practical test for every content decision: print the record on a thermal receipt printer in a truck. Does a technician still know what to do? That is the floor — and it is demonstrated live in the next section.

Canary metric

Any integration delivering zero records for 14 days is presumed broken until proven otherwise. A silently dead integration is the characteristic failure mode here and the hardest to notice.

Sizing question

How big is "None"?

A substantial share of small HVAC dealers run no FSM at all. If that share is large, the fallback inbox is the product for them — and Option C quietly converges with Option A at a smaller budget.

06 — Technician app

The primary design deliverable

Carrier owns this completely, and under Option C it carries the brand relationship the web portal surrenders. Phone, one hand, gloves on, bright sun or dark crawlspace, intermittent signal, customer watching.

‹Job · CP-4417
Screen a — the signature

Job brief

Headline, urgency word, and the bring list surfaced before the tech leaves the shop. Designed to be read in three seconds in a truck before the door opens. This is the difference between one visit and two.

Screen c

Check sequence

Probability-ranked, tickable, fully functional offline. Tap the checks in the prototype — offline is a designed visual state, not an error.

Screen e — strategically critical

Outcome capture

The only reliable ground truth in the entire architecture. It feeds prediction accuracy, proves value, and survives a partner refusing to return data. Must be completable in under 60 seconds, in gloves. If it takes longer it will not be filled in, and the strategy loses its only measurement instrument.

Everything else

Advanced detail, collapsed

The ~50 configuration fields survive, grouped by function — airflow & duct · staging & capacity · accessories · zoning · diagnostics · utility rates. Never the first thing on screen.

07 — Content

In the uncontrolled tier, the words are the entire interface

No layout, no colour, no interaction. Content is not decoration on this product — it is the product. Pattern: headline (what) → consequence (why it matters) → first checks (what to do) → bring (what it probably needs).

Voice rule

Front-load the noun

"Blower motor has faulted," not "A fault has been detected on the blower motor." Dispatchers scan the first three words.

Voice rule

Three words, not a scale

Today · This week · Watch. In a foreign system there is no legend and no brand palette. P1 carries nothing. Today carries everything.

Voice rule

No sentence needs a second one

Any line may be the last that survives truncation. Each must stand alone.

Voice rule

State confidence on predictions

A prediction arriving with no fault code behind it will be disbelieved by default. "Detected — high confidence, six weeks of performance data."

Voice rule

No Carrier-internal vocabulary

No "IDU," no "dissipation board," no model-family shorthand in the headline. Those belong in evidence.

Voice rule

Sentence case

Foreign systems will already be shouting. Carrier records should read as the calm thing on the screen.

08 — Disintermediation

Four countermeasures, honestly partial

The strategy's central risk is becoming an invisible data supplier. These are the defences. Design should implement them without making Carrier records look like advertising — a record that reads as marketing gets ignored.

01

Provenance never truncates

Diagnosed by Carrier Connected Portal · CP-4417. Quiet, factual, always present — verify it in the record lab at the 240-character floor.

02

The technician app is the relationship

Deep links from every syndicated record open the Carrier app. This is the one daily-use surface Carrier keeps, which is why §06 gets the design budget.

03

Tier merchandising travels

On a free tier a prediction is delivered as a redacted stub: the fact that a prediction exists, without the diagnosis. Silence sells nothing; a visible locked door does.

04

Outcome capture stays Carrier-side

Carrier retains the ground-truth dataset even where a partner returns nothing. This is also the only defence that cannot be negotiated away by a partner.

09 — Design direction

Quiet portal, bold app, typographically neutral payload

The remaining portal

The aesthetic of a status page

Quiet, dense, administrative, fast. No hero metrics, no counter tiles, no sparklines. Its thesis is reliability, not engagement. Its job is to be legible at a glance and then let the person leave.

Technician app

Where the boldness goes

Large type, high contrast, generous targets. Sunlight, gloves, one hand. Mono for serials sized to scan against a physical nameplate at arm's length.

Signature element

The job brief card

Headline, urgency word, bring list — read in three seconds before the door opens. Everything else in the app can be restrained; this is the moment that makes it feel like it is on the technician's side.

Content-as-interface

Plain-text specimens are comps

Deliver the record in unstyled monospace, proving hierarchy holds with no visual assistance. Review these with the same seriousness as a screen. If the specimen is unclear, no downstream styling will save it.

Offline

A visual state, not an error

Design what a cached record looks like and what pending sync looks like. Errors say what happened and what to do next.

Accessibility floor

Colour was never load-bearing

AA contrast, full keyboard operation, app usable at 200% text. Urgency is never colour-alone — conveniently already true, because in the uncontrolled tier there is no colour at all.

11 — Sequencing

Own what you own first

Phase 1

Build the payload

Grouping engine, plain-language content library, record schema, truncation ladder, plain-text specimens. No integrations. Shared prerequisite with Option A.

Phase 2

Own what you own

Ship the technician app against the new record, including outcome capture. No partner dependency. Delivers standalone value, establishes ground truth, de-risks everything after it.

Phase 3

One deep partner, done properly

A single reference integration with full read-back. Prove the loop end to end. Five shallow integrations teach nothing and are unmeasurable.

Phase 4

Cover the long tail

Fallback inbox, structured email, SMS, print/export for the no-FSM segment. Move earlier if that segment turns out to be large.

Phase 5

Scale and instrument

Additional partners at shallow tier, feed health monitoring, proof-of-value screen, prediction accuracy loop.

The failure mode

Starting at Phase 3. Announcing partner integrations before the content library is right ships raw codes into partner systems, trains dispatchers to ignore Carrier records, and burns integrations that will not be re-earned. Partner trust is spent once.

12 — Open questions

Answer before design finalises

  1. Does Carrier have the commercial leverage to require outcome read-back as a condition of the feed? Without it, half the lifecycle is invisible and the strategy is largely unmeasurable outside the technician app. The single most important question here — and a BD question, not a product one.
  2. What share of the dealer base runs no FSM at all? If large, the fallback inbox is the main product rather than an edge case, and Option C converges with Option A for that segment.
  3. Does the technician app have enough daily active use to carry the brand relationship? The entire disintermediation defence rests on this. If adoption is weak today, it must be fixed in Phase 2 or the strategy has no anchor.
  4. Can Pro tiers be merchandised inside a syndicated record, or does the business model need to shift toward per-record or data licensing?
  5. Who owns partner relationships — product, channel, or BD? Unclear ownership is the most common way strategies of this shape stall.
  6. Which FSM platforms cover the largest share of connected dealers, and what are their actual API capabilities? Tier assignment cannot be designed against assumptions.
